## Calculating relative atomic mass

-   The relative atomic mass of each element is calculated from the **mass number** and **relative abundances** of all the isotopes of a particular element
    
-   The equation below is used where the top line of the equation can be extended to include the number of different isotopes of a particular element present
    

$A_{r}=\frac{(%\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}A\text{}x\text{}\mathrm{mass}\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}A)\text{}+\text{}(%\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}B\text{}x\text{}\mathrm{mass}\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}B)}{100}$

-   So, if there were 3 isotopes present then the equation would read:
    

$\frac{(%\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}A\text{}x\text{}\mathrm{mass}\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}A)\text{}+\text{}(%\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}B\text{}x\text{}\mathrm{mass}\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}B)\text{}+\text{}(%\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}C\text{}x\text{}\mathrm{mass}\text{}\mathrm{of}\text{}\mathrm{isotope}\text{}C)}{100}$

#### Worked Example

The table shows information about the Isotopes in a sample of rubidium with 72% <sup>85</sup>Rb and 28% <sup>87</sup>Rb

| **Isotope** | **Mass Number** | **Percentage abundance** |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| 1 | 85 | 72 |
| 2 | 87 | 28 |

Use information from the table to calculate the relative atomic mass of this sample of Rubidium.

Give your answer to one decimal place.

**Answer:**

-   Relative atomic mass = $\frac{(72\times85)+(28\times87)}{100}$
    
-   Relative Atomic Mass = **85.6**
    

#### Examiner Tips and Tricks

Isotopes are easy to recognise from their notation as they have the same symbol but different mass numbers.

For example, the two stable isotopes of copper are <sup>63</sup>Cu and <sup>65</sup>Cu
